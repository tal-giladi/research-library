## Agent memory

### Build an LLM Wiki for Agent Long-Term Memory — Paul Iusztin / Alexey Grigorev
https://luma.com/dtc-b1dl

Paul Iusztin and Alexey Grigorev’s free hands-on DataTalks.Club workshop (Tue 8 Sep) builds an agent-maintained LLM wiki as a persistent second brain. The design turns scattered notes, GitHub repositories, transcripts, and web material into a structured Markdown knowledge base with index.md files, automatic cross-links, synthesized knowledge artifacts, and specialized skills for summarizing, categorizing, and reasoning; retrieval and auto-update loops keep it fresh as new information arrives. The payoff is a local AI Research OS that lets coding, research, and content agents carry context across sessions instead of restarting from scratch, so each interaction compounds the value of the memory layer.

## Industry / company building

### Why companies are becoming a series of loops — Anish Acharya (a16z)
https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-companies-are-becoming-a-series

In this Lenny’s Podcast conversation, a16z GP and former operator Anish Acharya argues that AI-era companies will be built as a series of compounding loops—each job function or product workflow repeatedly turns inputs into outcomes and improves with use—rather than as one fixed organization or product. He rejects the fear of a permanent underclass, frames the largest consumer opportunity as ‘/loop, make me happier,’ and says durable moats are discovered through usage rather than designed in advance; as AI makes capabilities more available, distribution becomes an increasingly important moat. He also describes ‘model sommelier’ judgment—matching the right model and tool to each task—and points to products such as Claude Code, Codex, Lovable, Replit, and Wabi as examples of the shift.

## Engineering blogs

### Research acceleration: The view inside OpenAI
https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/6/research-acceleration-the-view-inside-openai/

Simon Willison highlights OpenAI’s essay on recursive self-improvement (RSI) and the company’s internal use of coding agents by research teams. The post includes a chart whose daily AI spend per researcher stays near zero in February 2026, rises to roughly $50 in April and $150 by June, then accelerates sharply in late July and reaches about $600 by late August; Willison speculates that the late-July inflection may reflect employees gaining access to the model later released as GPT-6 Astra. The item matters as an operational signal that agentic engineering may have moved from experimentation to routine, high-volume research infrastructure, rather than being only a benchmark or product claim. It does not, however, establish that extra spend caused productivity gains: the chart is a reported usage/cost metric, and the Astra explanation is explicitly speculation.
